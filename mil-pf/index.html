MIL-PF: Multiple Instance Learning on Precomputed Features for Mammography Classification

Nikola Jovišić, Milica Škipina, Nicola Dall'Asen, Dubravko Ćulibrk

Frozen foundation encoders, precomputed representations, and a lightweight MIL head for scalable mammography classification.

arXiv Code

Abstract

Modern foundation models provide highly expressive visual representations, yet adapting them to high-resolution medical imaging remains challenging due to limited annotations and weak supervision. Mammography, in particular, is characterized by large images, variable multi-view studies and predominantly breast-level labels, making end-to-end fine-tuning computationally expensive and often impractical.

We propose Multiple Instance Learning on Precomputed Features (MIL-PF), a scalable framework that combines frozen foundation encoders with a lightweight MIL head for mammography classification. By precomputing the semantic representations and training only a small task-specific aggregation module (40k parameters), the method enables efficient experimentation and adaptation without retraining large backbones.

The architecture explicitly models the global tissue context and the sparse local lesion signals through attention-based aggregation. MIL-PF achieves state-of-the-art classification performance at clinical scale while substantially reducing training complexity. We release the code for full reproducibility.

Method Overview

A decoupled pipeline for efficient weakly supervised mammography classification

A frozen foundation encoder extracts patch-level and global visual representations and a compact attention-based MIL head learns how to combine view-level context with sparse lesion evidence for final breast-level prediction.

Overview

The central idea is to move the expensive part of training out of the optimization loop. Instead of repeatedly fine-tuning a large visual backbone on high-resolution mammograms, MIL-PF extracts features once with a frozen encoder and performs downstream learning on those stored representations.

This shifts experimentation toward the aggregation layer, where a compact MIL head can focus on combining local lesion evidence with broader tissue context across views. The result is a much cheaper training setup that is still appropriate for weakly supervised breast-level prediction.
